The Project Manager Field Guide

You are training to become an AI Solution Architect. You will not spend your career hand-building project plans, WBS trees, or status decks — AI will draft most of them in seconds. Your job is to direct, evaluate, and approve what the AI produces. Working example

The example initiative: CareSignal AI

Every document in this guide is built against one concrete, believable initiative so you can see how each PM concept plays out in practice — not as an abstract template, but as a real plan an AI Solution Architect would have to sign off on.

The organization

Crestwood Regional Health Network is a nonprofit system of 6 acute-care hospitals and 40 outpatient clinics across the Midwest United States, roughly 1.1M annual patient encounters and $2.1B in annual revenue.

The problem

Crestwood's 30-day all-cause readmission rate runs 16.8% against a CMS-benchmarked target of 13.5%, driven largely by incomplete discharge coordination and care-transition plans built ad hoc by nurses under time pressure. The gap costs the network roughly $9.4M/year in Hospital Readmissions Reduction Program (HRRP) penalties — before counting the human cost of avoidable returns to the hospital.

The initiative

CareSignal AI — a readmission-risk scoring model integrated into Epic that flags high-risk patients 24–48 hours before discharge, auto-drafts an individualized transition-of-care plan for nurse and care-coordinator review, and routes the patient to a care coordinator with a scheduled follow-up.

Scope

A 9-month build (September 2026 – May 2027) culminating in a 2-hospital pilot, then network-wide rollout. Sponsor: Chief Clinical Officer. Business owner: VP of Care Coordination.

Why project management exists

Project management exists to close the gap between what was promised and what actually gets delivered, on what date, for what cost. Left unmanaged, that gap produces initiatives that quietly slip a quarter at a time, budgets that are "mostly fine" until they aren't, and go-live dates nobody can defend when the steering committee asks why.

As an AI Solution Architect, generative AI will draft your charter, your WBS, your Gantt chart, and your status report in seconds. That collapses the writing cost of project management to nearly zero — and raises the judgment cost to nearly everything. You are no longer graded on how fast you can build a project plan. You are graded on whether you can look at an AI-generated plan and know, specifically, whether the critical path is real, whether the RAID log has owners, and whether the RAG status on the dashboard is backed by numbers or by optimism.

The 20% that matters
You do not need to become a full-time project manager. You need the ~20% of the discipline that lets you direct the AI ("build the WBS to 100% of scope, mark the critical path, and put a two-week buffer before the pilot gate"), evaluate its output ("this RAID row has no owner — reject it"), and approve what gets baselined. That 20% is what the rest of this Foundations section teaches.

The scope / schedule / cost triangle

Every project is bounded by three levers — scope, schedule, and cost — with quality sitting at the center, constrained by all three. Push on one corner and at least one other corner moves. A plan that promises to expand scope, hold the date, and hold the budget is not an aggressive plan — it is an unfunded promise.

The discipline

Name, in writing, which corner of the triangle is fixed and which corners are the flex variables the steering committee will trade against. A plan that treats all four dimensions as fixed simultaneously has not made a decision — it has deferred one to the first missed date.

CareSignal AI's fixed corner

For CareSignal AI, scope and quality are fixed — the bias audit, clinical validation gate, and HIPAA compliance requirements are non-negotiable given the patient-safety and regulatory stakes. Schedule and cost are the flex variables the Steering Committee trades against a slip, exactly as it does in the Status Report's Decisions Needed.

Reviewer's flag
If an AI-generated plan doesn't say which corner is fixed, it hasn't made a trade-off decision — it has hidden one. Ask for it explicitly before approving the charter.

Work Breakdown Structure (WBS)

A Work Breakdown Structure decomposes the total scope of a project into discrete, ownable work packages. The 100% rule is what makes it a control tool rather than a wish list: the work packages at any level must sum to exactly 100% of the scope defined one level up — no gaps, no overlaps, no "miscellaneous" bucket absorbing the difference.

Review habit
When an AI hands you a WBS, add up the percentages before you read a single work package description. If they don't sum to 100%, the decomposition is incomplete or double-counted — send it back before you look at anything else. See the CareSignal AI WBS for a package structure that sums correctly.

Critical path

The critical path is the longest sequence of dependent tasks from project start to project finish. It determines the earliest possible finish date — and it is the only sequence in the schedule with zero float: a one-day slip anywhere on it is a one-day slip to the whole project. Every other task has some slack to absorb a bad day without moving the finish date.

Why it matters to an architect

An AI-generated Gantt chart that doesn't name its critical path is decoration, not a schedule. If you can't say which task, delayed by one day, delays the whole program, you cannot manage the schedule — you can only watch it.

CareSignal AI's critical path

Charter Approved → Discovery Complete → Integration Env Ready → Risk Model v1 Trained → Clinical Validation Gate → Pilot Go-Live → Pilot Review Gate → Rollout Complete. Named and held in the Milestone Timeline.

Buffer, not optimism
A healthy critical path carries an explicit schedule buffer at its riskiest handoff — for CareSignal AI, a 2-week buffer sits between the Clinical Validation Gate and Pilot Go-Live, absorbing exactly the kind of integration delay logged as RISK-02 in the RAID Log.

Milestones & gates

A milestone is a zero-duration marker of a significant event — "Discovery Complete," "Model Trained." A gate is a milestone with teeth: a decision point with written exit criteria that must be met before work is authorized to continue. The difference matters. A milestone without exit criteria is a date on a slide; a gate is a control.

Common AI-generation failure
AI-drafted timelines routinely produce milestone dates with no exit criteria attached. "Clinical Validation Gate — Feb 26" is a date. "Clinical Validation Gate — PASSES only if fraud-score demographic disparity is below threshold and Compliance signs off" is a gate. Reject the former.

RAID

RAID is a single log covering four distinct categories that AI-generated plans routinely blur together: a Risk is something that might happen and would hurt if it did; an Assumption is something taken as true without proof, which becomes a risk the moment it's questioned; an Issue is a risk that has already happened and now needs active management; a Dependency is an external factor the plan needs but does not control.

The non-negotiable rule
Every RAID row needs exactly one named owner and a date — not "the team," not "TBD." A risk with no owner is a risk nobody is managing, no matter how well it's described. See the fully-owned example in the CareSignal AI RAID Log.

RACI

A RACI matrix assigns one of four roles to every stakeholder against every activity: Responsible (does the work), Accountable (owns the outcome and answers for it — exactly one per activity), Consulted (gives input beforehand, two-way), Informed (told afterward, one-way).

The rule that gets violated most

Exactly one Accountable per row. Zero means no one owns the outcome; two or more means a decision has no tiebreaker and will stall in a disagreement. This is the single most common defect in an AI-generated RACI matrix.

Why it isn't the org chart

The Accountable owner changes by activity — a Compliance Officer may be Accountable for the bias-audit gate while a Data Science Lead is only Consulted, even though the Data Science Lead outranks no one on the org chart for that decision.

Agile vs. waterfall vs. hybrid

Waterfall sequences phases with formal gates between them — well suited to work with a hard regulatory or clinical-safety gate that cannot be iterated past. Agile delivers in short, timeboxed increments with continuous feedback — well suited to uncertain, discovery-heavy work like model development. Most real AI initiatives are hybrid: waterfall-style phase gates for irreversible, safety-critical decisions, with agile sprints inside the phases that build toward them.

CareSignal AI's shape
The program runs as ten waterfall-style WBS phases with hard exit gates (Clinical Validation, Pilot Review) — but the Risk Model and Transition Generator phases inside that structure are built in two-week sprints, tracked with a burndown. See Sprint 8 for the agile layer inside the phase-gated shell.

Velocity & burndown

Velocity is the average amount of work (in story points) a team completes per sprint, measured over several sprints — never trust a single sprint's number. A burndown chart plots remaining work against time within one sprint: an ideal line assuming steady progress, against the actual line the team is tracking. A team should commit to no more than its trailing velocity, plus a small stretch buffer it is not accountable for delivering.

Reading a burndown
Actual consistently above ideal isn't automatically bad news — it can mean the estimate was wrong, a dependency slipped, or scope crept mid-sprint. What matters is whether the gap is diagnosed, not just charted. See Sprint 8's burndown, which closes 4 points short and states why.

Status reporting

A status report exists to answer one question honestly: are we still going to hit the date, the budget, and the scope we promised? RAG status (Red/Amber/Green) is only useful when it's derived from numbers — Schedule Performance Index (SPI), Cost Performance Index (CPI), open high-severity risk count — not from how confident the reporting manager feels that week.

Red flag
A status report with no "Decisions Needed" section is not a status report — it's a newsletter. If the schedule or budget is at risk, the report must name the specific decision the sponsor has to make, and by when. See the CareSignal AI Status Report for both decisions currently open.

PM KPIs

Four numbers tell you more about a project's real health than any status color: SPI (Schedule Performance Index, EV/PV — below 1.0 means behind schedule), CPI (Cost Performance Index, EV/AC — below 1.0 means over budget for the work actually done), EAC (Estimate at Completion — what the project will actually cost, forecast from current performance), and VAC (Variance at Completion — BAC minus EAC, the projected overrun or underrun in dollars).
